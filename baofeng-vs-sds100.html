Baofeng UV-5R vs Uniden SDS100: Which One Should You Buy?

One costs about $25. One costs about $700. Both are radios you hold in your hand. The difference is what they can hear, and whether your town is analog or digital decides which one you need.

Price, and what you get for it

A Baofeng UV-5R costs about $25. It comes with a battery, a charger cradle, an antenna, and a belt clip. For that money you get a dual band analog FM radio with 128 memory channels. That is the whole deal, and it is a good deal for what it is.

A Uniden SDS100 costs about $700. The SDS150, its newer sibling, is about $950. For that money you get a true I/Q software defined scanner with a color touchscreen, a 5400 mAh battery, and the ability to hear P25 Phase I and Phase II, DMR, and NXDN, follow trunked systems, and handle simulcast. It is a different class of radio entirely.

The price gap looks absurd until you hear what each one does in a digital city. Then it makes sense.

What each one can hear

The Baofeng hears analog FM voice on VHF and UHF. That covers a lot of small town police and fire dispatch, county sheriffs, GMRS, MURS, NOAA weather, ham repeaters, and business channels. If your town is still analog, a Baofeng loaded with the right channels is a busy little radio.

The SDS100 hears all of that, plus everything digital: P25 Phase I and Phase II trunked systems, DMR, NXDN, and conventional digital channels. It follows trunked systems by talkgroup, so you hear the conversation instead of hunting for the frequency it jumped to. It also handles simulcast, the thing that makes most digital scanners go deaf in a simulcast city. The SDS100 was built for simulcast. That is the feature you are really paying for.

Neither radio can hear encrypted channels. When a department encrypts, that channel is gone for everyone, on any radio, at any price.

The full version of what a Baofeng can and cannot hear is on How to Use a Baofeng UV-5R as a Police Scanner.

Analog towns vs digital cities

Here is the question that decides it: what does your town use?

If your county is analog, the Baofeng wins on value by a mile. A $25 radio and a $4.99 channel file gets you listening tonight. There is no reason to spend $700 to hear the same dispatch.

If your city is digital, the Baofeng loses no matter what you do. It physically cannot decode a digital signal. You can program the frequencies in, and the radio will sit silent. In that case the SDS100 is not a luxury, it is the only radio that hears your town.

Most places are a mix. The fire department may still be analog while the police went digital. A Baofeng hears the analog half. An SDS100 hears all of it. Check your county on RadioReference before you buy either one. The Mode column tells you the truth: FM means analog, P25 or DMR or NXDN means digital, and an E means encrypted.

Scan speed and ease of use

The Baofeng scans slowly, a few channels a second, and it stops on anything with a signal. With 128 channels that is fine. It has no screen worth reading while scanning, just a channel number and a frequency. The menus are numbered 0 to 40 instead of named, and the factory manual is a bad translation. It is a $25 radio, and it feels like one.

The SDS100 scans far faster, follows trunked talkgroups, and shows you the agency, the talkgroup name, and the signal details on a color screen. It is programmed with Sentinel software on a computer, or with a service like Program My County that builds the programming for you. It is a much nicer radio to use, and it should be, for $700.

Both radios need programming to be useful. A scanner with no channels programmed is a paperweight at any price.

Battery life

The Baofeng wins here, and it is not close. The stock 1800 mAh battery lasts up to about 12 hours of listening, because an analog radio sips power. Extended batteries are cheap.

The SDS100 gets about 6 to 8 hours on its 5400 mAh battery. The software defined receiver that makes it so good at simulcast also eats power. That is the trade. If you run an SDS100 all day, carry a spare battery or a USB power bank.

Who should buy the Baofeng

Buy the Baofeng if your town is analog, if you want a first radio to learn on, if you want a cheap second radio for the car or the go bag, or if you mostly want to listen to GMRS, MURS, NOAA, and ham. At $25 it is the cheapest way into the hobby, and it is a real radio, not a toy.

And program it right. A Baofeng with the wrong channels in it hears nothing, and then people say Baofengs are junk. They are not junk. They are analog radios, and they hear analog channels fine. The Baofeng CHIRP Packs collection has ready-made channel files for St. George, New York City, Los Angeles, Chicago, Salt Lake City, Moab, and Las Vegas, plus a Nationwide Starter pack with GMRS, MURS, NOAA, and ham calling. $4.99 each, every channel receive only. The how to is on How to Program a Baofeng UV-5R With CHIRP.

Who should buy the SDS100 or SDS150

Buy the SDS100 or SDS150 if your town is digital, if you want to hear trunked police and fire, if you live in a simulcast city, or if you tried a Baofeng and heard nothing because your agencies went digital. It is the radio I use when a Baofeng cannot do the job.

The SDS150 is the newer model, about $950, with waterfall included and a GPS menu. The SDS100 is about $700. Both hear the same systems. If you buy one, this is my affiliate link for the SDS150: Uniden SDS150.

And if you want it programmed for your county, Program My County builds the programming for you for $24.95, or the City Scanner Packs collection has pre-built favorites lists for 38 cities, 8 theme parks, and 9 airports and attractions, $4.99 each.

The honest answer: many people end up with both

This is the part the comparison articles skip. The Baofeng and the SDS100 are not really competitors. They do different jobs. The SDS100 sits at home or in the car and hears everything digital. The Baofeng rides in a pocket, a go bag, or a glove box, costs almost nothing to replace, and hears the analog channels in town. A lot of scanner people own both, and they reach for whichever one fits the moment.

If you can only buy one, buy the one that hears your town. Check RadioReference first. Analog town, buy the Baofeng. Digital city, buy the scanner. That is the whole decision.
