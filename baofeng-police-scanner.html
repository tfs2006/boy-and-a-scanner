How to Use a Baofeng UV-5R as a Police Scanner

Yes, a $25 Baofeng can hear police and fire dispatch. It can also hear nothing at all, depending on where you live. This page tells you which one it will be in your town, and how to program the channels that are actually there.

What a Baofeng can hear, and what it cannot

The Baofeng UV-5R is an analog FM radio. It hears analog FM voice on VHF and UHF, which is still how a lot of police, fire, and EMS dispatch works in smaller towns and rural counties. It hears GMRS, MURS, NOAA weather, ham repeaters, and plenty of business and public works channels too.

What it cannot hear: anything digital. It cannot decode P25, DMR, or NXDN. It cannot hear a trunked system. It cannot hear an encrypted channel, and no radio you can buy can. It cannot receive AM aircraft band. If your town's police moved to a digital trunked system, the Baofeng will sit silent on those channels no matter what you program into it.

That is not a flaw in the radio. It is a $25 analog radio doing exactly what a $25 analog radio does. The trick is knowing what your town uses before you spend an evening typing frequencies in by hand.

Analog, digital, and encrypted, in plain words

Analog is plain old FM voice, the same kind of signal as a walkie talkie. A Baofeng hears it fine.

Digital is voice turned into data, like P25, DMR, and NXDN. It sounds like static or a buzz on an analog radio. You need a digital scanner to hear it, such as the Uniden SDS100 or SDS150.

Encrypted is digital voice scrambled with a key. Nobody can listen to it, not even with a $700 scanner. When a department encrypts, that channel is gone for listeners, full stop.

Most cities are a mix. The fire department may still be analog while the police went encrypted. A county sheriff may be analog while the city police are digital. That is why the honest answer to "can a Baofeng hear police in my city" is always "it depends on your city," and why a pack built for one city cannot be used for another.

How to find your local frequencies on RadioReference

RadioReference is the database almost every scanner owner uses. Here is the short version.

First, go to radioreference.com and open the Database. Pick your state, then your county. You will see a list of agencies and a list of trunked radio systems. Click an agency to see its frequencies. The Mode column tells you what you are looking at: FM or FMN means analog, and your Baofeng can hear it. P25, DMR, or NXDN means digital, and your Baofeng cannot. A mode marked with an E, or a talkgroup marked encrypted, means nobody can hear it.

Second, check the trunked systems for your county. If your police and fire live on a trunked system, look at the system type. P25 Phase I and Phase II, DMR, and NXDN are all digital. Some trunked systems still carry analog talkgroups, but most do not.

Third, write down the analog frequencies that matter to you: dispatch channels first, then tactical and fireground. Those are the ones worth programming.

Program it by hand, or load it with CHIRP

There are two ways to get channels into a UV-5R. By hand, you switch to frequency mode with the VFO/MR button, type a frequency on the keypad, then save it with Menu 27, MEM-CH. It works, and it takes about a minute a channel. A 73 channel pack would take you over an hour.

With CHIRP, you do it once on a computer. CHIRP is free software from chirpmyradio.com. You need a programming cable, the Kenwood style two pin cable that plugs into the side of the radio. Download from the radio first and save a backup, then import a channel file and upload it back. The whole job takes about 15 minutes, and the file does the typing for you. The full walkthrough is on How to Program a Baofeng UV-5R With CHIRP.

What to do when your police is digital

First, check what is still analog in your area. Fire and EMS often stay analog long after police go digital. County sheriffs, small town police, public works, schools, and hospitals are often analog too. A Baofeng loaded with the analog channels in your county can still be a busy little radio.

Second, do not buy a pack for a city where the headline agencies are digital and hope for the best. Every pack on this site says plainly what it does not hear, right in the listing. Read that part before you buy.

Third, if the channels you want are digital, you need a digital scanner. That is a different radio and a different price, and it is worth it if listening to your town matters to you.

Why you hear nothing on your Baofeng

The agency is digital.
Your Baofeng is analog only. A digital channel sounds like static or a buzz, or nothing at all. Check the Mode column on RadioReference.
The agency is encrypted.
Nobody can hear it, on any radio. Nothing to fix.
A receive tone is set wrong.
If a channel has a CTCSS or DCS receive tone set and the signal does not carry that tone, the radio stays silent. For listening, leave the receive tone off unless you know the channel needs it.
The squelch is too high.
Menu 0, SQL, set it to 1 or 2. A high squelch setting mutes weak signals entirely.
You are scanning with nothing saved.
A Baofeng with no memory channels has nothing to scan. Save at least one channel first, then press and hold the star key to scan.
