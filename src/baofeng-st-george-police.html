Can a Baofeng Hear Police in St. George, Utah?

Short answer: yes, for the dispatch that matters. St. George police, fire, and the sheriff moved to a digital P25 system, but the same dispatch is simulcast on analog VHF. That is exactly what a Baofeng hears.

The short answer

St. George police, fire, and the Washington County sheriff now live on the Utah Communications Authority (UCA) P25 system. A Baofeng UV-5R cannot hear P25. It is an analog FM radio, and P25 is digital voice.

Here is the part most people miss: the same dispatch is simulcast on analog VHF frequencies. Program those frequencies and the Baofeng hears the same calls the scanner hears, on the sheriff, St. George police, and St. George fire dispatch channels.

That is the honest shape of it in St. George. The Baofeng hears plenty, and it hears nothing digital. Read the next two sections before you program anything, so you know exactly which side of that line each channel falls on.

What else is around St. George

Ham repeaters, all with a 100.0 tone: 145.490 Utah Hill (Dixie ARC), 146.640 Webb Hill, 145.450 Toquerville, and 146.820 Utah Hill Intertie. Dixie National Forest, no tone: 171.550 West, 172.350 East, and 168.625 Air-Guard.

Then there is the universal set that works anywhere in the United States: 22 GMRS channels, 5 MURS channels, 7 NOAA weather channels, and the 2 ham calling frequencies. Those are in every pack in this series, St. George included.

What the Baofeng does not hear in St. George

Say it plainly so nobody buys the wrong thing. The Baofeng does not hear UCA P25 trunked traffic. It does not hear the Utah Highway Patrol. It hears no digital channels at all, and no aircraft band, because the UV-5R cannot receive AM airband.

The VHF channels above are simulcasts of the same dispatch, and the SDS150 hears the P25 side. If the channels you want are the digital ones, you need a digital scanner. That is a different radio and a different price, and it is worth it if hearing the P25 side matters to you. How to get these channels in the radio

Two ways. By hand, switch to frequency mode with the VFO/MR button, type a frequency on the keypad, set the receive tone with Menu 11, turn the transmit tone off with Menu 13, then save it with Menu 27. Do the one-time setup first: Menu 1, STEP, set to 2.5K, and Menu 0, SQL, set to 1 or 2 so weak signals break through. Hold the star key for about two seconds to scan once channels are saved. The full button-by-button walkthrough is in my free manual: Baofeng UV-5R Plus Manual, Program It by Hand. Or watch the video version: Baofeng UV-5R Plus, Program It by Hand.

With CHIRP, you do it once on a computer. CHIRP is free software from chirpmyradio.com, and you need a programming cable. Download from the radio first and save a backup, then import a channel file and upload it back. The whole job takes about 15 minutes. The full walkthrough is on How to Program a Baofeng UV-5R With CHIRP.

Listening is legal, transmitting is not, on this radio

Listening to police, fire, and EMS on a Baofeng is legal in almost every state. Transmitting on public safety frequencies without authorization is illegal, and the UV-5R holds no Part 95 grant, so GMRS and MURS are listen only on this radio even with a GMRS license. Ham is the only transmit path, and that needs a license. Keep Menu 13, T-CTCS, set to OFF, and never press PTT on a public safety channel. The full answer is on Can You Use a Baofeng UV-5R on GMRS?.
