Baofeng UV-5R Frequencies Worth Programming

Every frequency worth putting in a Baofeng UV-5R, with the numbers. All of these are listen only in this guide. Program them, scan them, and hear what is actually out there.

The seven NOAA weather channels

NOAA weather radio broadcasts 24 hours a day, and your local transmitter uses one of these seven frequencies. Scan through them and keep the strongest one. Listen only, you cannot transmit on these.

Set the NOAA channels to skip while scanning, so the weather broadcast does not stop your scan every few seconds. In CHIRP that is the Skip column, set to S.

GMRS channels 1 to 22, listen only on this radio

GMRS is the 22 channel UHF service a lot of families, off roaders, and neighborhood groups use. FRS radios share these same frequencies, so programming GMRS channels 1 to 22 also covers FRS. Do not program FRS separately.

Listen only on the UV-5R. The radio holds no Part 95 grant, so transmitting on GMRS with it is not legal, even if you hold a GMRS license. The license fixes the operator, not the radio. The full story is on Can You Use a Baofeng UV-5R on GMRS?.

Channels 15 to 22 are repeater outputs. Program the output frequency as a simplex listen channel and you will hear the repeater traffic. Do not program the repeater inputs, the 467 MHz frequencies 5 MHz up, because you would only hear the handheld talking into the repeater, and only if you are close to it.

MURS channels 1 to 5

MURS is five license free VHF channels, used by businesses, farms, and some neighborhood groups. No license is needed to transmit on MURS, but the UV-5R still cannot legally transmit on it, because the radio holds no Part 95 grant. Listen only on this radio.

Ham calling frequencies

Two frequencies every ham knows. The 2 meter national FM simplex calling frequency is 146.520. The 70 centimeter national FM simplex calling frequency is 446.000. Hams call on these, then move to another frequency to talk. Listening needs no license. Talking needs an amateur license, Technician or higher.

If you hear a local repeater, its output frequency is worth programming too. RepeaterBook lists them by area. Program the output frequency as a simplex listen channel, the same way as the GMRS repeater outputs above.

Marine channel 16

Marine channel 16, 156.800, is the international distress and calling channel for boats. If you live near water, it is worth having in the radio. It is FM voice on VHF, and the UV-5R receives it fine. Listen only. Hailing and distress traffic only, no chatter.

Local police, fire, and EMS, and how to find yours

The nationwide lists above are the same everywhere. The channels that make a Baofeng fun are the local ones, and those are different in every town. Look yours up on RadioReference: open the Database, pick your state and county, and look for analog FM frequencies. The Mode column tells you what you are looking at. FM or FMN means analog, and your Baofeng can hear it. P25, DMR, or NXDN means digital, and it cannot. A mode marked with an E means encrypted, and nobody can hear it.

The full how to is on How to Use a Baofeng UV-5R as a Police Scanner.

What the UV-5R cannot hear

The UV-5R is analog FM only. It cannot hear P25, DMR, or NXDN digital systems, it cannot follow a trunked system, it cannot hear encrypted channels, and it cannot receive AM aircraft band. If the channels you want are digital, you need a digital scanner. That comparison is on Baofeng UV-5R vs Uniden SDS100: Which One Should You Buy?.
