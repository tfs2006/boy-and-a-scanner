Baofeng UV-5R for Beginners: What to Do the First Night You Own One

You just unboxed a Baofeng UV-5R. The manual is a bad translation, the menus are numbered instead of named, and you want to hear something tonight. Here is the first night, step by step, no jargon.

Charge it and put the antenna on

First, screw the antenna on snug. It is the long black stick in the box. The radio will not receive much without it, and transmitting without an antenna can damage the radio, so make this step one.

Second, charge the battery. The UV-5R comes with an 1800 mAh battery and a drop in charger cradle. Put the radio in the cradle, plug it in, and wait for the light to turn green. A full charge takes a few hours. While you wait, do not turn the radio on and off a dozen times. Let it charge.

Third, attach the belt clip if you want it, and keep the earpiece in the box for now. You will not need it tonight.

Listen first, talk later

The best first night with a Baofeng is a listening night. You do not need a license to listen. You do not need to talk to anyone. Turn the radio on with the volume knob on top, and just hear what is out there.

One warning before you press the big button on the side: that is the push to talk button, PTT. Do not press it. On a UV-5R, pressing PTT transmits, and transmitting needs a license and the right frequency. Listening is free and legal. Talking is not. Keep your finger off PTT tonight.

Your first channel: NOAA weather

NOAA weather radio is the easiest first win. It broadcasts 24 hours a day, it is strong almost everywhere in the United States, and it needs no license to listen to. There are seven NOAA channels: 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525, and 162.550. Your local transmitter uses one of the seven. Scan through them and keep the strongest one.

To tune one in, press VFO/MR until the screen shows frequency mode instead of a channel number. Type 162.550 on the keypad. You should hear a weather broadcast, a calm voice reading the forecast on a loop. If you hear static, try the next frequency down the list. When you find the strong one, save it. That is your first channel, and the radio already feels useful.

The full list of seven NOAA channels, plus GMRS, MURS, and ham calling, is on Baofeng UV-5R Frequencies Worth Programming.

The buttons that matter

A UV-5R has a lot of buttons. You only need five of them tonight.

VFO/MR. Switches between frequency mode, where you type a frequency, and channel mode, where you use saved channels. You will live in channel mode once you have channels saved.

A/B. Switches between the top line and the bottom line on the screen. The radio watches both, but it only transmits on the one with the little arrow next to it. For listening, just make sure the arrow is on the line you are watching.

MENU. Opens the settings. The menus are numbered 0 to 40 instead of named, which is why the factory manual reads so badly.

Up and down arrows. Step through channels in channel mode, or step through frequencies in frequency mode.

The star key, held down. Starts scanning through your saved channels. Press any key to stop.

Everything else can wait until tomorrow.

How to save a channel by hand

Here is the whole trick, and it is the same for every channel you will ever save.

First, press VFO/MR until you are in frequency mode. Second, type the frequency on the keypad, for example 162.550 for NOAA weather. Third, press MENU, then 27, then MENU again. That is MEM-CH, the save menu. The screen asks which channel slot to save it in. Pick a number, press MENU to confirm, then press EXIT. Done. Switch to channel mode with VFO/MR and your saved channel is there.

Two settings to fix while you are in the menus. Menu 0, SQL, is squelch. Set it to 1 or 2. A high squelch mutes weak signals. Menu 21, MDF-A, sets what channel mode shows on the top line. Set it to CH so the screen shows channel numbers instead of frequencies.

The license question, answered plainly

Listening needs no license. You can listen to NOAA, GMRS, MURS, ham repeaters, police, fire, and anything else your radio can hear, with no license at all.

Transmitting is different. To talk on ham frequencies you need an amateur license, Technician or higher. To talk on GMRS you need a $35 GMRS license from the FCC, good for 10 years, no test, and it covers your immediate family. Here is the catch that surprises people: even with a GMRS license, you still cannot legally transmit on GMRS with a UV-5R, because the radio holds no Part 95 grant. The license fixes the operator, not the radio. Ham is the only transmit path on a UV-5R, with a license. The whole story is on Can You Use a Baofeng UV-5R on GMRS?.

What to program next

After NOAA, the channels worth adding are GMRS channels 1 to 22, the five MURS channels, and the two ham calling frequencies, 146.520 and 446.000. They are the same everywhere in the United States, and they are the channels you will actually hear things on while you learn the radio. The frequencies are all on Baofeng UV-5R Frequencies Worth Programming.
