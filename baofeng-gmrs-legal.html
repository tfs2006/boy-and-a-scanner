Can You Use a Baofeng UV-5R on GMRS?

Short answer: listen yes, talk no. You can hear every GMRS channel on a UV-5R with no license at all. Transmitting on GMRS with a UV-5R is a different story, and it is not the one people want to hear.

The short answer

Listening to GMRS on a Baofeng UV-5R is legal, and you need no license to do it. Transmitting on GMRS with a UV-5R is not legal, and a GMRS license does not fix it. The license covers the operator. The radio itself has to be certified for GMRS, and the UV-5R is not.

That is the whole answer. The rest of this page is why it works that way, and what to do instead.

What Part 95 means, in plain words

The FCC sorts radios into services, and each service has its own part of the rules. Ham radio is Part 97. GMRS is Part 95E. FRS is Part 95B. MURS is Part 95J. A radio has to be certified under the part for the service you use it on.

The Baofeng UV-5R is certified as an amateur radio, Part 97. That is why it can legally transmit on ham frequencies, with a ham license. It holds no Part 95 grant at all. That is why it cannot legally transmit on GMRS, FRS, or MURS, no matter what license you hold. The radio was never certified for those services.

This is not a technical limit. The UV-5R can physically transmit on the GMRS frequencies. It is a paperwork limit, and paperwork is what the FCC enforces.

Listening is legal, transmitting is not, on this radio

Listening is legal. You can program all 22 GMRS channels into a UV-5R and listen to families, off roaders, and neighborhood groups, with no license and no paperwork. The frequencies are on Baofeng UV-5R Frequencies Worth Programming: channels 1 to 7 on 462.5625 to 462.7125, channels 8 to 14 on 467.5625 to 467.7125, and channels 15 to 22 on 462.5500 to 462.7250.

Transmitting is where it stops. Pressing the push to talk button on a GMRS channel with a UV-5R is transmitting on a service the radio is not certified for. A GMRS license does not change that, because the license authorizes you, not the radio. A lot of people do it anyway, and a lot of people will tell you nobody enforces it. That may be true in practice. It is still not legal, and I am not going to tell you otherwise.

The same rule covers FRS and MURS. FRS shares the GMRS frequencies, and MURS is five VHF channels, 151.820, 151.880, 151.940, 154.570, and 154.600. The UV-5R can hear all of them. It cannot legally transmit on any of them.

The $35 GMRS license, and what it does and does not fix

A GMRS license costs $35, paid to the FCC. It is good for 10 years. There is no test. One license covers you and your immediate family. You apply for it on the FCC website, and it is the easiest license in radio.

What it does: it authorizes you to transmit on GMRS with a certified GMRS radio, at up to 5 watts on a handheld, with repeater access. That is the whole point of GMRS, a family radio service with real range.

What it does not do: it does not make an uncertified radio legal. A UV-5R with a GMRS license in your pocket is still a UV-5R. The license fixes the operator, not the radio. If you want to talk on GMRS, you need a certified GMRS radio and the license together.

Which Baofengs are actually GMRS certified

Baofeng does make GMRS radios. They are different models from the UV-5R, built and certified for Part 95E. If you want to stay with Baofeng and talk on GMRS legally, these are the ones to look for.

Baofeng GM-15 Pro. A 5 watt GMRS handheld, Part 95E certified, FCC ID 2AJGM-GM15PRO. About $25 to $35. The cheapest legal seat at the GMRS table. NOAA weather, USB-C charging, repeater capable.

Baofeng UV-5G and UV-5G Plus. The GMRS versions of the UV-5R shape, 5 watt, Part 95E certified, repeater capable, CHIRP programmable. The Plus has a bigger battery and a color screen. About $30 to $40.

Baofeng UV-9G. The waterproof one, IP67 rated, 5 watt, Part 95E certified. Same GMRS job, tougher radio.

Baofeng G11S and GM21. Simpler GMRS handhelds, Part 95E certified, repeater capable. The G11S is 3 watts and very easy to use.

When you shop, check the listing for Part 95E certification or the FCC ID. A radio that just says "GMRS frequencies" in the title is not the same as a certified GMRS radio. The certification is the thing that makes it legal.

What to do if you already own a UV-5R

You have three honest options, and they are all good.

First, keep the UV-5R as a listener. Program the GMRS channels receive only and hear everything in your area. That is what the radio is good at, and it costs you nothing more.

Second, get a ham license and talk on ham. The UV-5R is a real ham radio, and the Technician license is a 35 question test you can study for in a weekend. Then the radio you already own is fully legal to transmit on, on ham frequencies.

Third, buy a certified GMRS radio for talking and keep the UV-5R for listening. A GM-15 Pro costs about the same as the UV-5R did. One radio for GMRS, one radio for everything else. A lot of people end up here.

FRS and MURS, same story

FRS is license free, and it shares the GMRS frequencies, channels 1 to 22. That does not make a UV-5R legal to transmit on FRS. FRS radios have to be certified under Part 95B, with a fixed antenna and 2 watts max. The UV-5R is none of those things. Listen yes, talk no, same as GMRS.

MURS is license free too, five VHF channels, no repeaters, 2 watts max. Same story. The UV-5R can hear MURS fine. It cannot legally transmit on it, because it holds no Part 95 grant.

The pattern is simple, and it covers the whole radio: the UV-5R listens to everything analog in its range, and it only transmits legally on ham, with a license. Everything else is listen only.
